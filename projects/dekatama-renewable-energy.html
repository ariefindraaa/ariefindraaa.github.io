PLTS Planning and BCS, PJUTS & PATS Field Installation

An engineering internship at PT Deka Surya Energi Utama (Dekatama) spanning four locations: PLTS technical planning in Yogyakarta and Bandung, battery charging system installation in Lombok, and multi-point solar street lighting and solar water pump installation in Sumbawa.

Project overview

Dekatama is a renewable energy EPC contractor, and this internship covered both halves of what an EPC actually does: the planning work that happens at a desk, and the installation work that happens on a roof or at the side of a road.

Over the internship the work moved across four locations — technical planning in Yogyakarta and Bandung, then field deployment in Lombok and Sumbawa — which made the handover between design intent and site reality very visible.

Problem statement

Solar projects fail in the gap between the drawing and the site. A sizing concept that ignores the available roof area, the cable route, or the logistics of getting equipment to an island location does not survive construction.

The internship therefore focused on producing planning documents that are actually buildable, and on learning what the field does with them.

Scope & my role

  • Prepared technical planning for PLTS Hotel Sahid JWalk, including sizing concepts and wiring plan support.
  • Prepared technical planning for the PLTS cluster project in Bandung.
  • Supported single-line diagram (SLD) preparation for the planned PV systems.
  • Joined the Battery Charging System (BCS) installation in Lombok, carried out in collaboration with Universitas Mataram.
  • Supported multi-point PJUTS (solar public street lighting) installation in Sumbawa: mounting, wiring, and commissioning.
  • Supported PATS (solar water pump) installation in Sumbawa.
  • Took part in site survey, equipment checking, and functional testing across the field deployments.

Method & workflow

  1. Survey and assess. Review the site conditions, available area, and load requirement for each project.
  2. Develop the sizing concept. Match PV capacity, conversion equipment, and storage to the site requirement.
  3. Document the design. Support SLD and wiring plan preparation so the field team has something buildable to work from.
  4. Prepare the deployment. Check equipment, assemble the required materials, and plan the logistics to the installation site.
  5. Install on site. Erect mounting structures, fit modules and equipment, and complete DC/AC wiring for BCS, PJUTS, and PATS units.
  6. Commission. Verify wiring, energise the system, and confirm correct operation before handover.

Lessons learned

  • Planning and installation teach each other. Doing the field work after drawing the wiring plan changed how I draw wiring plans.
  • Island logistics drives the schedule more than the electrical work does — material readiness is the real critical path.
  • Commissioning discipline is what separates a system that works on handover day from one that works a year later.
  • Collaborating with a university partner on the Lombok BCS work showed how industry and academic teams split responsibilities on a shared deployment.
