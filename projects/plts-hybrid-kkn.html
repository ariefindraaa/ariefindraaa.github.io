1.3 kWp Hybrid Off-Grid PLTS — KKN-PPM UGM

A community energy deployment during KKN-PPM UGM: survey, installation, and commissioning support for a 1.3 kWp hybrid off-grid solar system at Masjid Pesantren Nurul Huda in Lampung Selatan, delivered in collaboration with PT PLN Nusantara Power.

Project overview

KKN-PPM is UGM's community service programme, and this deployment was its flagship technical output: a hybrid off-grid solar system installed at Masjid Pesantren Nurul Huda so the facility keeps running during grid interruptions.

Alongside the technical work, the project involved coordinating equipment and transport logistics for the team — a real constraint when installing hardware in a rural location far from the supplier.

The project was published as programme news on the Teknologi Rekayasa Elektro UGM website in November 2025.

Problem statement

The pesantren depends on a grid supply that is not always continuous, which disrupts lighting and essential loads at a facility that is used daily by the community.

The solution had to be affordable, maintainable by people who are not electrical engineers, and robust enough to keep working without specialist attention after the student team leaves.

Scope & my role

  • Took part in the site survey: existing installation, load profile, roof and mounting conditions, and panel orientation.
  • Supported the system configuration for a 1.3 kWp hybrid off-grid arrangement — PV array, solar charge controller / inverter, and battery storage.
  • Acted as equipment and transportation coordinator for the KKN team, which covered procurement logistics and getting the hardware to site.
  • Carried out physical installation work: mounting structure, PV module installation, and DC/AC wiring.
  • Supported commissioning: polarity and continuity checks, voltage measurement, and functional testing of the system.
  • Contributed to the solar public street lighting (PJU tenaga surya) installed as part of the same programme.
  • Helped brief the local caretakers on basic operation and routine maintenance.

Method & workflow

  1. Survey the site. Assess the existing electrical installation, the daily load, and the available roof area and orientation.
  2. Configure the system. Define the 1.3 kWp hybrid off-grid arrangement — array size, charge controller / inverter, and battery capacity against the load profile.
  3. Coordinate equipment and logistics. Organise procurement, packing, and transport of the hardware to the site in Lampung Selatan.
  4. Install the structure and modules. Erect the mounting system and fit the PV modules at the correct orientation and tilt.
  5. Wire the system. Run and terminate the DC and AC cabling between array, controller, battery, and distribution.
  6. Commission and test. Verify polarity and continuity, measure voltages, energise the system, and confirm that the connected loads run correctly.
  7. Hand over. Brief the pesantren caretakers on operation, safety, and routine maintenance.

Lessons learned

  • In rural deployments, logistics is engineering. A missing connector costs a day, so equipment coordination is as critical as the electrical design.
  • Designing for the people who stay behind matters more than designing for peak performance — simplicity wins when nobody on site is an engineer.
  • Commissioning checks are worth doing slowly and in order; catching a polarity mistake before energising saves hardware.
  • Working with an industry partner like PLN Nusantara Power showed how community programmes are structured and reported in practice.
