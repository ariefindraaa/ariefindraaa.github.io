RTCT Pertamina — Electrical Verification & Commissioning

A practical industry internship in the Production & Control Division of PT Patra Jasa on the RTCT Pertamina Daan Mogot project in Jakarta — covering cable sizing verification, a series of commissioning tests, and project progress reporting.

Project overview

This internship sat on the construction side of electrical engineering: verifying that what is installed matches what was designed, then proving it works through a structured sequence of tests before handover.

The work spanned the electrical distribution chain from the 20 kV medium-voltage supply down to the 400/230 V low-voltage installation, and extended into the commercial side of project control through BoQ and AHSP preparation.

Problem statement

On a building project, electrical design and electrical reality drift apart. Cable sizes get substituted, routes change, and the only way to catch that before handover is systematic verification followed by testing.

Project control has the same problem in a different form: progress has to be measurable, which means quantities and unit prices have to be documented in a way stakeholders can actually read.

Scope & my role

  • Verified cable sizing for the distribution from the 20 kV medium-voltage system down to the 400/230 V low-voltage installation.
  • Participated in megger (insulation resistance) testing of the electrical installation.
  • Participated in air-conditioning system commissioning.
  • Participated in hydrant pressure testing.
  • Participated in emergency alarm system testing.
  • Participated in lift load testing.
  • Prepared Bill of Quantities (BoQ) documentation for the project.
  • Prepared AHSP (unit price analysis) material.
  • Built Power BI dashboard material for project progress reporting.

Method & workflow

  1. Study the design. Review the electrical drawings and specifications for the distribution system from 20 kV down to 400/230 V.
  2. Verify cable sizing. Check the installed and specified conductor sizes against the load requirement and acceptable voltage drop.
  3. Prepare for testing. Coordinate with the site team on test scope, sequence, and safety before each commissioning activity.
  4. Execute the test programme. Take part in megger testing, AC commissioning, hydrant pressure testing, emergency alarm testing, and lift load testing.
  5. Record the results. Document test outcomes and note any item requiring follow-up.
  6. Support project control. Prepare BoQ and AHSP material for the scope of work.
  7. Report progress. Build Power BI dashboard material so project progress is visible to stakeholders at a glance.

Lessons learned

  • Verification is a habit, not an event — checking cable sizing against the actual load is quick and catches expensive mistakes early.
  • Commissioning is as much about sequence and coordination as it is about instruments; the test only works if the right people and systems are ready.
  • BoQ and AHSP work made it clear how engineering decisions translate directly into project cost.
  • A dashboard changes the conversation. Presenting progress visually got far more engagement than the same numbers in a table.
