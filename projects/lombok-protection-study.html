Lombok Island Power-System Protection Study

An operational study of the Lombok power system: load-flow analysis of the 150 kV network, voltage-profile improvement using a 75 Mvar capacitor bank, and protection coordination based on overcurrent (OCR) and distance relay settings.

Project overview

This study looks at the Lombok island transmission system from two angles at once: whether the network operates within acceptable voltage limits, and whether its protection scheme clears faults selectively.

The two questions are connected. Reactive compensation changes the power flow and the voltage profile, which in turn changes the fault levels that relay settings have to be coordinated against.

✅

Tooling note: this protection study was carried out in ETAP 21, not DIgSILENT. DIgSILENT PowerFactory is used only in the final project on transient stability.

Problem statement

A relatively long 150 kV island network tends to suffer voltage-profile problems at the electrically remote end, especially under heavy loading, and reactive support is the usual remedy.

At the same time the protection scheme has to stay selective: the relay closest to a fault should operate first, with upstream relays acting only as backup. Settings therefore have to be verified against the actual short-circuit levels of the network as it is operated.

Scope & my role

  • Modelled the Lombok 150 kV system in ETAP 21, including generation, lines, and substation configuration.
  • Ran load-flow studies to evaluate the bus voltage profile and line loading.
  • Analysed the effect of a 75 Mvar capacitor bank as reactive compensation to improve the voltage profile.
  • Performed short-circuit analysis to obtain the fault current levels used as the basis for relay settings.
  • Determined overcurrent relay (OCR) settings: pickup current, curve characteristic, and time multiplier.
  • Determined distance relay zone settings for the protected line sections.
  • Verified selectivity and backup behaviour through the time-current coordination curves.

Method & workflow

  1. Build the system model. Represent the Lombok 150 kV network in ETAP 21 with its generation, transmission lines, and substations.
  2. Run the base-case load flow. Evaluate the voltage profile and line loading of the network as operated.
  3. Study the reactive compensation. Apply the 75 Mvar capacitor bank and re-run the load flow to quantify the improvement in the voltage profile.
  4. Compute fault levels. Run short-circuit analysis at the relevant buses to obtain the currents the protection has to respond to.
  5. Set the overcurrent relays. Determine pickup, curve type, and time multiplier settings so each relay protects its own zone with an appropriate grading margin.
  6. Set the distance relays. Define the protection zones and their reach for the relevant line sections.
  7. Check coordination. Plot and review the time-current curves to confirm the correct operating sequence between primary and backup protection.

Lessons learned

  • Protection settings are only as trustworthy as the short-circuit study behind them — the network model has to be right first.
  • Reactive compensation and protection cannot be designed in isolation; changing one changes the conditions the other has to handle.
  • Reading coordination curves is a fast way to catch selectivity mistakes that are hard to see in a table of settings.
  • Documenting the assumption behind every setting makes the study reviewable by someone who was not involved in building the model.
